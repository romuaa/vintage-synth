# Design decisions log

Decisions made during implementation, with the reason for each. Newest milestone first.
Anything marked **(review)** is a judgement call that the product owner may want to change. Items marked "confirmed by owner" were reviewed and accepted.

## B-Side S2b-2: band-limited waveform rendering (2026-11-15, all review)

Contract: `docs/architecture.md` 8.4.2, 8.4.4, 8.4.5, 8.9.4, 8.15. Design as built: "RENDERING" at the end of
`source/bside/chip/Chip.h`. The reference emulator was not run for this step (only committed reference
features were used); its source and tables were not opened.

| # | Point | Reading taken (reason) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What `render` covers of 8.4.4** ("adds the output after volume, before the board filter", 8.4.7). | S2b renders oscillator -> waveform converter -> x envelope converter, summed; routing, filter, oscillator 3 off, voice DC, volume and board filters wait for S3. `render` adds the plain sum of the three voices (d (v) minus the converter's mid-scale, times the envelope), so S3 can take the voices apart later without changing the digital side. **(review)** |
| 2 | **Splitting a sample at a write.** 8.4.7 has `render (out, n)` in whole samples; 8.16 renders "to `nextTickCycle()`". | Added `renderUntil (out, maxSamples, untilCycle)`: it stops inside a sample at the requested cycle, so a write made then lands at its exact cycle; the next call completes the sample. `render (out, n)` stays (whole samples). A write right after `render` (at a sample boundary) is placed at its cycle too, with values taken up to one cycle later. **(review)** |
| 3 | **The ideal waveform** (what "band-limited form of the chip's waveform" means when the chip itself is sampled at 1 MHz). | The continuous line through the digital accumulator: saw wraps and triangle folds where the line crosses them, pulse edges one cycle after the line crosses the width (the comparison is one cycle late, 8.15.2 (c)), so the rendered waveform leads the chip's digital output by 0 to 1 cycle. Without that, the chip's own 1 MHz sampling (edge jitter of up to one cycle) would alias at about -50 dB, and the budgets of 8.4.5 could not hold. **(review)** |
| 4 | **Sync resets** happen in the digital core at the cycle after the source's top bit rose. | Rendered at the source's crossing (sub-cycle), predicted from the source's line and confirmed by the digital reset; the target's line then runs offU = its advance between the two ahead of the digital accumulator. Placing the reset at the digital cycle aliases at about -40 dB (measured during development). A prediction the digital core does not confirm within three cycles (the sync-chain exception, 8.15.2 (d)) is corrected band-limited at the next sample end and counted (`renderCorrections`). **(review)** |
| 5 | **Model A converter** (8.4.4: "an unterminated ladder with a ratio of about 2.2"). | A resistor-ladder network solved for its bit weights (`RenderTables.cpp`), ratio 2.2, unterminated, for the 12-bit waveform and the 8-bit envelope converter; **Model B ideal** (ratio 2, terminated: binary weights), as "near-ideal" in 8.4.4. Our own model, not fitted further. **Asserted against the reference features** (`RenderReferenceTests.cpp`, `[dac]`, our tolerances, no number in 8.9.4): envelope converter levels per frame (`a_env_dac_*`, every counter value, frames from 24 on where both lie above -40 dBFS, one gain): rms 0.13 dB (A) / 0.09 dB (B), worst frame 0.70 / 0.36 dB (allowed rms 0.3, worst 1.0); waveform converter (`a_dac_*` saw 110 Hz, saw 1 kHz, pulse 110 Hz): harmonics 2..8 within 0.1 to 0.3 dB (allowed 0.6). The first frames are left out: power-on and the first gate carry S3's voice DC and board high-pass (the earlier "constant 0.4 dB" for Model B was the gain fitted with those frames in). A Model A converter made ideal fails the test (mutation). **Finding for S3:** the reference's triangle at 110 Hz has a second harmonic at -34 dB (A) / -40 dB (B) and a fourth at -43 / -52 dB; a converter that is linear in its bits cannot make even harmonics on a triangle (its two halves are complementary codes), so this is a nonlinearity after the converters (mixer or output stage); the triangle segment is not compared. **(review)** |
| 6 | **Converter-bent triangle and saw** (8.4.5 permits mip tables for shapes the DAC bends). | Closed form instead: d (v) = g v + sum e_i bit_i (v); every index bit whose square wave stays below half the internal rate is rendered as its own square wave with exactly placed edges, the faster ones as their mean (a band-limited step where the set changes with the frequency). Exact for sync, ring and writes, which a table read is not. **(review)** |
| 7 | **Combined waveforms** (8.4.5 names mip-mapped tables). | Rendered as exact band-limited staircases: our fitted shapes change value at only 51 to 664 of the 4096 cells per period, so every change is a BLEP. Mip tables were built first: free-running they met the budget, but the pulse gate (and ring, sync, pull-down) multiplies a band-limited table by a hard edge and aliased at -50 to -75 dB, which BLEP/BLAMP corrections of the jump and slope cannot remove. Cost: up to about 2.6 million steps per second per oscillator at the register maximum (Model A saw+pulse). **(review)** |
| 8 | **Model A pull-down** (8.15.2 (c) step 4) in the renderer. | The chip pulls in the first step whose landing cell (whole cycles) has output bit 11 clear, the pulse comparison (made on the landing before) included. From a rise the line is pulled where it enters such a cell, the gate taken as the chip compared it on the landing before the rise (whole cycles; the chip's own comparison when a write followed that step). After a write inside the upper half the chip's landings are followed on the digital accumulator (`renderLanding`, also when the line, less than a cycle ahead, has already wrapped; above one cell per step the landings skip cells) and the pull-down is scheduled at that landing. (Following the landings from every rise too was built and measured: no test and no harness count changed, so it was removed.) The one-cycle output glitch the chip shows at that cycle (the upper-half value for one cycle) is not rendered in the oscillator's own output (staircase-level detail); as oscillator 1's ring or sync source it is (row 26). The pulse gate is re-read from the new line one cycle after a pull-down. **(review)** |
| 9 | **Noise.** "sample-and-hold at the noise clock with the contract's treatment". | Each noise clock (a shift or a fill under the test bit) is a band-limited step at its exact cycle; noise with another waveform (write-back) renders the digital output cycle by cycle, a step wherever it changes (the core steps every cycle there anyway). |
| 10 | **How aliasing is measured** (8.4.5: "as for Vintage"). | At the internal rate, in 0 .. min (20 kHz, 20/48 x host rate), for every internal rate of 4.4 (88.2, 96, 128, 176.4, 192, 352.8, 384 kHz), bin-centred with a rectangular window (Smooth frequencies put the fundamental on a bin), against the fundamental; for sync, ring and combined waveforms against the strongest legitimate component, as Vintage's sync gate does (their fundamental can be weak; Model A saw combinations play at 2 f0 after the pull-down). B-Side has no decimator yet (S4): what it adds is measured with it. Sync: Vintage's ratios (1.37, 2.71, 0.71, 1.0031, 1.9969); no oscillator above the register maximum (3.85 kHz). Write discontinuities: a write every tick at its exact cycle, 7-term Blackman-Harris window, every component more than 9 bins from the signal's own lines. **(review)** |
| 11 | **A second, stronger BLEP kernel (coordinator decisions, 2026-11-15; the budget is not relaxed).** | With the shared kernel (8 zero crossings, Kaiser beta 8, float) Model A's combined waveforms missed "<= -96 dB up to 2 kHz" by up to 10 dB, and (independent review) narrow pulses (width <= 64 or >= 4032) reached -85 dB and Model B combined shapes with sync -88 dB: that kernel attenuates what folds into the band by at most 85.5 dB (`[.render-diag]`). `common/dsp/BlepTable.h` now takes zero crossings, Kaiser beta and the sample type as parameters (`BasicBlepTable<Z, betaTenths, Sample = float>`; `BlepTable` is Vintage's unchanged 8 / 8.0 / float). **Chosen: 8 zero crossings, Kaiser beta 12, double precision**, for every step of an oscillator playing a **pulse or a combined waveform, on both models** (and the writes that enter or leave one), read with a second-order correction of the table's linear interpolation (without it the nearly silent Model A triangle+saw+pulse stays 4 dB over). Searched: 8/10 to 16/12 (bin-centred rows, Model A combined) and 8/11, 8/12, 8/13, 10/12, 10/13, 12/13 (the second measurement, narrow pulses): 8/12 meets every row of both measurements with >= 5.3 dB to spare (the narrowest pulses at 88.2 kHz, 3.85 kHz: a pulse narrower than one chip cycle, two full steps that nearly cancel); 8/11 left those at 3.7 dB. Double precision did not change the figures (the floor was aliasing, not table precision) and is kept because the steps of a narrow pulse cancel to about -66 dBFS. Same length as the short kernel: the latency stays 8; triangle, saw, noise and the per-cycle modes keep the short kernel (row 25 for how that is tested); the fade of a deselected output takes the long kernel (row 23). **(review)** |
| 12 | **Latency.** | The chip adds `Chip::kLatencySamples` = 8 internal samples, the long kernel's window (which every step shares; both kernels have 8 zero crossings, so the short kernel's steps are not delayed further). Measured: a write's step lands at exactly cycle / cycles-per-sample + 8 (deviation below 6e-7 with each step's own kernel), through `renderUntil` and through `render` followed by a write (the step sits at the write's cycle, up to a cycle before the sample's end). At the host rate: 8 / factor samples (4 at 2x, 2 at 4x, 8 at 1x) plus S4's decimator. |
| 13 | **Agreement with the reference** (8.9.4). | Noise third-octave spectra (`a_noise_a`, `a_noise_b`, 8 frequency settings each) within 0.18 dB of the reference after one gain per trace (allowed 1.5 dB), rendered at 192 kHz through test stand-ins of the board filters (8.15.7) and a test decimator. The gain is fitted because the absolute scale is S3's. The other analog tolerances need the filter and the output stage (S3) and stay skipped. |

"Typical" and "worst" understate. **The S4 gate (8.5: 16 voices <= 5 % of a core at 48 kHz 2x, filter included) allows about 31 ns per chip at 96 kHz.** Saving candidates (review): an idle / event-free fast path per oscillator (about 35 of the silent chip's 54 ns are overhead); the Model A converter terms through a table of the deviation, or dropping the bits below 6; a higher-resolution long table instead of the second-order correction; a pitch cap or tables for combined waveforms above 3.85 kHz. **(review)** |
| 15 | **Sync and ring from a Model A saw combination (review H1, defect fixed).** | The joint line simulation that predicts sync resets and ring flips did not apply the Model A pull-down, so such a source (triangle+saw, saw+pulse, triangle+saw+pulse) rose in the simulation and reset or flipped its target every period (sync: -44 dB against the chip's stream at 220 Hz, ring: inverted, +6 dB). Now the simulation pulls the source down where `renderSweep` does (the first upper-half cell whose output has bit 11 clear, the pulse gate included; `StepTable::firstClear`): a source pulled at once (all our fitted saw combinations are, at cell 0x800) never rises, never syncs, and its top bit never shows; a source pulled later (the S2a placeholder shapes, at 0xc00) rises, syncs, and flips its ring target back at the pull. Tested against the chip's own output stream (`[stream]`). **(review)** |
| 16 | **The pulse gate after a phase jump.** | After a sync reset the one-cycle-late comparison needs no re-read (the next edge computed from the new line already lies one cycle on: the re-check removed there); after a Model A pull-down landing above `slope` (a later pull-down) it does, and stays, tested with the placeholder shapes (a mutation without it fails). |
| 17 | **Small corrections (review); what `renderCorrections()` guarantees.** | The output starts on the chip's value at activation and after `reset()` (the samples before the first one are pre-filled with it; was an unbandlimited step 0 -> about -0.8); the Model A converter bits switch with hysteresis (row 22); `prepare` and the constructor always fetch both BLEP tables, and a Chip must be constructed and prepared off the audio thread; the render tables (about 180 KiB) are built in place; the physical output peak is asserted at 1.7 (three voices of +-0.5 plus about 12 % band-limited overshoot; 1.671 measured over random patches; was 4.0). **Corrections, exactly:** `renderLargeCorrections()` (above 1e-4 of a period) is asserted 0 over 1 200 patches of the review's harness kind (`[corrections]`: writes 1 to 40 cycles into a sample, four write kinds); `renderCorrections()` is asserted 0 over 200 random patches without writes (every waveform incl. the noise combinations, sync, ring, no sync chains, both models, every rate) and over 120 random write schedules (about 70 writes each: controls, frequency, width, test bit; `[schedule]`). | 20 | **Predictions across a write (second review, item 4).** | At a write the renderer keeps or drops what it predicted and the chip has not done yet: a predicted pull-down always ends there (the line goes back to the digital phase; if the chip has not pulled yet, it decides with the new controls through `renderLanding` / `renderPullDown`); a predicted sync reset whose sync bit the write cleared is dropped. offU (the sub-cycle time between a rendered and a digital sync reset, held as a phase) is rescaled with the frequency at a frequency write, and so is the line of an unconfirmed sync reset, so the two lines stay less than a cycle apart in time (held as a phase, the offset grew to many cycles at a lower frequency, and the chip's pull-downs then came "late"). The state continues only from a triangle or saw; from another mode it is seeded afresh from the frequency and no switch is counted (it was seeded from the other mode's terms). Measured: the reviewer's vibratos (+-3 to +-25 %, around 80, 90 and 100 % of the limit, 2 s each) at most one switch; a glide 150 -> 525 -> 150 Hz at 96 kHz three (bit 3 and bit 4 dropped, bit 4 back). `[hysteresis]`. **(review)** |

## B-Side S2b-1: our own combined-waveform tables (2026-11-15, all review)

Contract: `docs/architecture.md` 8.4.5, 8.15.2 (c), 8.15.8 (owner decision: our own fits, never copied).
The reference emulator was only run (through the adapter, on our own traces); its source and tables
were not opened.

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **What is observable.** The read register shows output bits 11..4 only; bits 3..0 are not observable through the trace interface at all. They also never reach the digital state (8.15.2 uses out bits 11..4 for the read value and the write-back, bit 11 for the Model A pull-down), so the digital comparison needs exactly the observable bits. Bits 3..0 are the model's extrapolation and matter only for the audio (S2b rendering). **(review)** | The `.bsspec` features are too coarse to recover single low bits; no audio is stored. |
| **Model: a resistor network per combination and chip model** (CombinedWaveforms.h): each selected waveform drives every bit line through its own pull-up or pull-down conductance, neighbouring lines are coupled, a selected pulse ties every line to one common node, conductances and threshold have per-bit slopes; node voltages solved exactly, bit = voltage >= threshold. At most 11 parameters per combination (pulse-free ones use 8). Tables computed from the parameters at the first prepare. **(review)** | Few parameters, physically motivated (shared bit lines), predicts all 12 bits. Simpler kernels (distance-weighted average with a threshold) left 2 to 4 times more errors; a feedback (latching) extension gave no gain. |
| **Fit quality (full tables, pulse width 0; entries whose 8 observed bits all agree / observed bits wrong / max and RMS error of the observed value in rd1B steps), at the committed fixed point.** Model A: tri+saw 99.78 % / 0.027 % / 32 / 0.71; tri+pulse 97.27 % / 0.37 % / 128 / 13.8; saw+pulse 95.75 % / 0.70 % / 128 / 11.5; all three 98.71 % / 0.20 % / 128 / 12.4. Model B: tri+saw 98.78 % / 0.20 % / 128 / 2.7; tri+pulse 91.99 % / 1.20 % / 128 / 20.4; saw+pulse 92.48 % / 1.02 % / 128 / 18.2; all three 98.07 % / 0.27 % / 128 / 12.6. Pulse + noise (Tpn, all noise samples): Model A 96.4 % / 0.45 %, Model B 94.9 % / 0.71 %. **The pulse combinations fit worst** (Model B tri+pulse and saw+pulse about 92 %). Per plan: `bside_shapefit report tests/bside/shapes`; tolerances in `tests/bside/trace/ShapeDataTests.cpp` (fully agreeing plans pinned at 1.0, plans of at most 128 samples at the achieved count, sweeps 0.2 % below, bit fractions just above the achieved). **(review)** | Honest limit of a few-parameter model; a per-entry table would fit exactly but would be copied data, which Q5 rules out. |
| **Pulse width enters only as the mask** of 8.15.2 (c) step 3: at pulse widths 0x400 and 0xa00 every sample whose comparison is low reads 0 and the others agree with the width-0 shape within the fit error (one shape per combination, not per width). **(review)** | Measured (`s_pw_*`), as the contract assumes. |
| **Noise with two or more of triangle, saw and pulse: constant shape 0** (both models). 96 (A) / 48 (B) samples each read 0, except two Model B triangle+pulse+noise samples (noise output 0xf8 at index 0x87a reads 0x80; 0xfd at 0x7e8 reads 0xf8; fit 95.8 %). **(review)** | Measured (`s_noise_b/d/e/f_*`). |
| **Pulse with noise is indexed by the noise output (contract decision A, 2026-11-15).** The data first: a fixed noise output (0xfc, after two shifts from a full refill) reads the same value at 63 indices (`s_pn_noise_*`), and 96 (A) / 83 (B) distinct noise outputs, each at up to two indices and across all pulse-with-noise plans, never give two values (`s_pn_index_*`, `s_noise_c_*`). So the wording holds. Tpn is the network with the noise output (bits 11..4) driving the bit lines in the saw slot plus the pulse node; step 3's AND with the noise output stays. The `c_c_*` traces now agree at every exempt checkpoint (were 10 of 100 differing with the constant). | Measured; the constant of the first hand-back reached 20.8 % / 50 %. |
| **Fit procedure: a fixed point.** `bside_shapefit fit` runs deterministic rounds (one thread per table, own generator; 6 000 random starts, pattern search from the best 16, random joint moves) from the committed parameters until a round changes nothing; a round accepts strict improvements of the wrong-bit count only, so a round from a fixed point returns it unchanged. `bside_shapefit verify` checks that (about 1 min; a developer check, not in ctest). The committed fit took 4 rounds. Noise constants: per-bit majority. Hexadecimal literals; the parameters are `constexpr`-built and `constinit` (no static-initialisation order can see them unset). | Review finding: the first hand-back was not a fixed point (refit improved two tables). |
| **Floating point.** Every decision the constraints leave to the network keeps a margin of at least 2.7e-6 from its threshold (tested against kMinMargin = 1e-6), and the SHA-256 of both models' tables (incl. Tpn) is pinned in `bside_unit`; the GCC Linux run computes the same hashes. **(review)** | Tables computed at run time instead of stored. |
| **Measurement design** (`tests/bside/shapes/ShapePlan.h`): sweeps at frequency 0x1000 with a checkpoint per cycle (Model A reads the same step, Model B its latch, one step later); the Model A saw combinations' upper half and index 0 by one-step spot samples between sync resets (the pull-down sends a sweep back below 0x800); noise samples after a refill under the test bit, with random frequency and delay so the index and the noise output are independent, skipping steps with a noise shift. Index and masks are taken from the reference's own checkpoints and the design is checked. Only `.bstrace` and `.bsref` are committed (4.5 MB); the `.bsspec` outputs are not used. | Smallest data that covers every entry. |
| **`bside_independent`**: the independent model (IndependentModel.h, not ours to edit) has no table interface and keeps the S2a placeholder; its tests now build in their own executable whose sources are all compiled with `BS_CHIP_DEFAULT_SHAPES=placeholder`, so chip and model agree on everything except the table values (tested against the reference instead). Interface it would need: a shape callback or a `const ShapeTables&` (16 x 4096 values per model) passed to `Model::reset`, used where it computes `shape` today; then the executable can go. **(review)** | The alternative, editing that file, is forbidden to whoever edits the chip. |

## B-Side S2c: patch program engine (2026-11-15, all review)

Where `docs/architecture.md` 8.6 (with 8.13 and 8.14) is silent, ambiguous or unbuildable as written, the engine

| # | Point | Reading taken (reason) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What a lane command writes.** 8.6.2 says a tick's writes are listed "with equal cycles, in that order" but not which registers a command touches. | A lane command always writes (literally, each time it runs, even when the value is unchanged): `wave` writes the frequency (only when it has a pitch) then control; `pitch` the frequency; `gate on/off` control; `adsr` registers 5 then 6; `set`/`sweep`/`pingpong` the pulse width (low byte then high nibble); `cutoff` registers 0x15 then 0x16; `res`/`route` register 0x17 (the other half taken from the current effective value); `mode`/`volume` register 0x18. | 2 | **Registers that follow the host** (8.6.2 "ownership"). | Per field: frequency, envelope pair, control bits, gate, pulse width, cutoff, resonance, routing, mode, volume. The engine writes a host-driven group when its effective value differs from the last value it wrote ("sync"): at every tick, at note-off, and when the caller calls `sync` (host parameter change). | 3 | **Order inside a tick** (8.6.2 gives only the lane order). | Restart writes; then the three wave lanes' rows, then the host-driven wave registers of oscillators 1 to 3 (whether an oscillator the host leaves off needs its frequency depends on the controls the wave lanes just wrote, so every wave lane runs first); then for pulse lanes 1 to 3: the lane's rows, then that oscillator's pulse-width sync; then the filter lane, then the filter sync. | 4 | **Idle oscillators.** | An oscillator is active when `oscN.wave` is not Off or its wave lane has rows. | 6 | **Clean's direct operation** ("the rate counter and divider are set to 0 directly"). | A sink call (`clearEnvelopeTimers`) at the note's cycle before anything else, when the voice did not get the full lead (retrigger, steal, no restart section, short idle) under Clean. `ChipProgramSink` answers it with `Chip::clearEnvelopeCounters (n)` for **all three** oscillators of the voice's chip: 8.6.4 names no oscillator and the restart concerns the whole voice (reading chosen; review). | 7 | **Stolen voice** (8.6.4 end). | Clean and Precondition: `resetChip`, then the restart writes (mode not off), at the note cycle (the caller passes the cycle after the fade); Authentic does nothing special. `ChipProgramSink` answers `resetChip` with `Chip::powerOnKeepingTime()`: power-on state, the chip's cycle count keeps running. After the reset the engine's register shadow is zero, as the chip's. |
| 8 | **Retrigger without a restart section** (not in 8.6.4). | Under Clean and Precondition a gate that is still high is cleared at the note cycle before tick 0, so the chip always sees an attack edge. | 9 | **`firstwave`.** "control := firstwave plus gate". | For every active oscillator at tick 0 (not under Authentic): control := (firstwave without gate) + gate while the note is held; envelope and frequency come from the host (the table's own values arrive with row 0 at tick 1); the wave lanes (only) wait one tick. (review) |
| 10 | **Tick clock.** "P is read at block start; a change applies from each voice's next tick". | The next tick's cycle is fixed when the previous tick runs (cycle + P at that moment). A change of P therefore applies from the tick after the one already scheduled. The caller gives P (`tickPeriodCycles`, pure function: Frame cycles by the clock (19 656 below 1 003 988 Hz, else 17 095), `round`-half-up everywhere, clamped 256 .. 2^30). |
| 11 | **Row timing details.** | A row with delay D >= 1 executed at tick t makes the next row run at tick t + D; a `sweep x n` adds at ticks t .. t + n - 1 and the next row runs at t + n; `pingpong` steps every tick from its own tick on until a release jump: the direction turns at a limit before the step (a value that starts on a limit does not repeat it), a step that would pass a limit is cut to it, so the value stops exactly on a limit and reverses there; a lane that runs past its last row ends like `end`; `repeat` counters are per row and reset when the note starts and when a repeat falls through. (review) |
| 12 | **Note-off** (8.6.2). | A tick on the same cycle runs first (the caller's order, 8.13 two bursts); then, per slot in lane order: a lane with a `release` label jumps there (also if it had ended, and leaving a sweep or ping-pong) and runs its burst; a wave lane with rows but no label clears its gate. **Once the note is off the table can no longer raise the gate**: every later control write has its gate bit cleared (waveform, sync, ring and test bits still apply, so a drum's waveform sequence plays out through the release without a second attack) (architect decision, review of S2c). | 13 | **Macros and resolution.** | `macro.pw_offset` and `macro.cutoff_offset` are added after the lane or host value and the sum clamped to 0..4095 / 0..2047, for lane and host values alike; `macro.sweep_depth` multiplies every sweep and ping-pong step, the lane's position is kept as a real number (a step of 0.5 at depth 0.5 is not lost). A non-finite host value is replaced by a safe default, never written. |
| 14 | **Frequency formula.** | Register value = 440 x 2^((m - 69) / 12) x 2^24 / clock with m = host semitones (+ the lane's relative offset, or the lane's absolute note alone). The host's semitones already contain key, glide, bend, vibrato, coarse and fine and unison detune (8.5); the program engine adds only the lane's part. |
| 15 | **Real time and robustness.** | `ProgramEngine` owns an 8 KB program copy and the lane state, no pointers and no heap; calls are noexcept and never allocate (counted in the property test, including over-aligned `new`). **Precondition: every program passed to `noteOn` / `enterIdle` has passed `validate()`** (the reader's output always does); debug builds assert it. Release builds bounds-check the indices the engine follows (lane and row ranges, command ranges, lane kind of each command: a wave command in the filter lane, a ping-pong outside a pulse lane, ... are ignored) so a corrupt program cannot read or write outside the engine; its musical behaviour is unspecified. A 128-row backstop per burst keeps a zero-delay loop from hanging. |
| 16 | **Chip sink time** (`ChipProgramSink`). | The chip keeps its cycle count through a steal (`powerOnKeepingTime`), so `cycle()` is the chip's cycle and equals the engine's time: a caller renders `engine.nextTickCycle() - sink.cycle()`. (An earlier version kept an offset because `reset()` zeroed the cycle count.) |

### Bit-exact rule with our own combined-waveform tables (orchestrator, 2026-11-14, review)
- With "follow, own tables" the fields that a combined-waveform shape reaches (the oscillator 3
  read register, the noise register after a write-back, on Model A an oscillator synced through a
  pulled-down top bit) cannot equal the reference bit for bit. **Chosen: those fields are exempt
  from the exact comparison while they depend on a shape; everything else stays exact**
  (architecture 8.15.8). Alternatives not taken: a tolerance on digital fields (a noise register
  that differs in one bit is simply a different sequence, so a tolerance means nothing), or exact
  comparison (possible only with the reference's tables, which the owner decided against).
- Consequence the owner should know: on Model A, hard sync from an oscillator that plays a
  combined waveform may fire at different moments than in the reference, as far as our fitted
  shape differs from it. If that turns out audible on real tunes, the remedy is "follow fully".

## B-Side S1c: table and trace formats (2026-11-14, all review)

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **A row ending in `jump` or `repeat` costs no tick: its default delay is 0** (an explicit `ticks N` is allowed on it). `end`, `sweep` and `pingpong` rows always store delay 1 and reject `ticks`. | 8.6.2 says a jump "continues at the label (same tick)", and the arpeggio example is described as "one note per tick"; with a default delay of 1 on `jump loop` it would idle every fourth tick. | **Every cycle of rows without delay is rejected, `repeat` included** (a `repeat` row has two edges: its target and the next row; the check runs only when nothing else is wrong with the table). **Guarantee: any accepted table runs at most 64 rows (`kMaxRowsPerTick`, the lane's row count) in one tick of one lane, from any start row (the note start, after a delay, the `release` label) with any state of the repeat counters.** The rows that run without a delay form an acyclic graph, so a tick follows a path of distinct rows that ends at the first row that waits, stops or sweeps. The runtime's 64-rows-per-tick cap is therefore only a backstop. | Review follow-up: the first reading (a repeat is not endless) accepted nested `repeat a 255` rows over a `ticks 0` row, which can run more than 10^9 rows without time advancing. The model test (`tests/bside/unit/RowsPerTick.h`) checks the bound on every accepted table of the fuzz runs. |
| **The canonical text always reads back, and `validate()` says so:** the reader rejects a row whose canonical line (4 spaces of indent, `, ` between commands, numbers in register units, generated label names, ` ticks N`) is over 200 bytes ("the canonical form of this row is N bytes long") and a table whose canonical text is over 32 KB; `validate()` also checks at most 16 jump targets (plus `release`) per lane and those two limits. The alternative, lifting the line limit for the writer's output, would make the canonical text unreadable by the reader. |

| **Errors:** syntax and range errors are found line by line; undefined, duplicate and other-lane labels, labels naming no row and the loop check come after the whole text has been read, so they follow the other messages (the first 8 are kept). A label that is the last thing of its lane is an error ("names no row"). | Label targets can be defined after their use. |
| **Trace file:** writable registers 0x00–0x18 (the two analog inputs and the read registers are not writable); register and value are exactly two hex digits; `clock` 1 000–100 000 000 Hz; header words in any order but before the first write; `end` last. **The 64-byte line limit applies to the statement (bytes before `#`); a whole line with its comment may be 256 bytes.** Numbers may have leading zeros (the digit limit counts significant digits). | The contract's own example has comment lines longer than 64 bytes, which a plain 64-byte line limit would reject. |
| **Trace and reference limits are tied together:** at most 100 000 writes and 49 999 periodic checkpoints per trace, so at most 150 000 checkpoints (writes + periodic + the end), and a reference file holds one line of at most 107 bytes per checkpoint, which fits its 16 MB limit (`static_assert`s in the headers). Each writer refuses output its own reader would reject. The contract's 10^6 writes would need a 214 MB reference file. | Review follow-up: a valid 200k-write trace needed a 20.8 MB reference that `readReference` rejected. |
| **Checkpoint schedule:** one checkpoint after every write; periodic ones at N, 2N, ... strictly before `end`, taken before the writes of that cycle; one at `end` after all writes. A trace may ask for at most 49 999 periodic checkpoints (see the row above). | 8.9.2/8.9.4 say "every N cycles (and always after each write)" without ordering at equal cycles; a total count is needed to bound the reference file and the replay. |
| **`.bsref` / `.bsspec` layouts (the contract names the content but not the grammar):** see `source/bside/trace/ReferenceFormat.h`. Field ranges: 24-bit accumulators, 23-bit noise registers, 8-bit envelope counters, envelope phase 0–2, 15-bit rate counters, divider counters 0–255, two read registers. Spec: one `frame` line per 25 ms hop (level plus 23 bands, 0.1 dB, -1200..300), at most 100 000 frames, at most 64 `segment` lines of 120 bins at 20 Hz × 2^(k/12). Hashes: 64 hex digits for the trace, 40 or 64 for commits. | Lines stay short (the level envelope is per frame instead of one long line), every field is bounded, and nothing but numbers and hashes is stored (no names, no audio). |
| **Comparer:** "level ±0.5 dB" is the overall level of the whole render (power mean of the frame levels), not each frame; cells at or below -80 dB in both are ignored (as in the render harness); noise third octaves are derived from the 1/12-octave bins by power-averaging groups of four, bands with centres 50 Hz–16 kHz. | **Replay is a template over the chip** (setup and snapshot callables) and the chip interface it needs is `advanceCycles`, `write`, `cycle`. `replay()` takes the trace file's bytes and refuses a reference whose SHA-256 or schedule does not match. | The real chip arrives in S2; the driver is proven now against a stub in the tests. |

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Fixed output trim −6.02 dB (× 0.5), multiplied into the master gain.** The loudest chord (Dual, 32 voices, velocity 127, dry, default master −6 dB) now peaks at **−2.57 dBFS** (was +3.45); moderate effects −0.45 dBFS (was +5.57); extreme effects still reach the clamp without the limiter (+12.04 dBFS, was the same). | Master volume range (−60 … +6 dB) and default (−6 dB) unchanged. | No strong reason to change them; the trim alone gives the headroom, and existing sessions keep their master setting. |

| **Both are session settings like master volume:** saved with the host session, not stored in programs, tones or banks; loading or initialising a program leaves them alone and changing them does not mark the program modified. | Least surprising: an output-stage setting the player chose for the session should not change when browsing sounds. | The ceiling is continuous and shown in 0.1 dB ("0.0", never "−0.0"). | A 0.1 dB interval snapped stored values, so a session did not restore the exact normalised value. |

## VCF slope switch on sounding voices (2026-11-13)

(review)

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **A VCF slope change crossfades the output between the stage-2 and stage-4 taps over 5 ms on every voice that is sounding** (held or releasing); idle voices and notes starting from idle take the slope at once. Outside the 5 ms the plain tap is used, so the steady sound is bit-identical (all render-regression scripts compared bit for bit). This applies to a hand flip of the switch as well as to a program change. | The two taps differ in phase and level, so switching between them stepped the output (0.18–0.33 on a held sine chord against its own 0.018). 5 ms is the parameter-smoothing time used elsewhere; the crossfade is a gain ramp between two correlated signals, so it has no step of its own. This does not smooth the switch value itself (the "never smooth switches" rule): the filter is in one mode or the other, only the output moves between them. |
| **Other switches are left as they are.** Measured on a held chord and in a program apply, waveform, range, sync, VCO-2 mode, ENV-1 polarity, VCF envelope source, ENV-1 key follow and chorus mode cause no step larger than the sound's own. | A waveform switch jumps the oscillator to the new shape at its current phase; the jump is no larger than the new waveform's own edges and is low-passed by the VCF, so it is not heard as a click. Not band-limited, so it can alias once; left as is unless the owner hears it. |

## Cross Mod aliasing (owner decision after the study, 2026-11-12)

- **The current Cross Mod sound stays as the instrument's sound ("Classic").** The owner compared
  it by ear with a corrected version at musically typical settings: the difference was almost
  inaudible, and where audible the corrected version "takes the edge off"; it also costs about
  40 % more CPU for voices using cross-mod.
- **Quality rule, refined:** the −100 dB alias gate applies to all waveforms and to hard sync.
  **Cross Mod in Classic mode is exempt** and documented: about −36 to −80 dB at one octave with a
  saw, square or pulse modulator, worse at higher depths and modulator pitches; sine and triangle
  modulators measure about −87 dB at one octave.
- **A "Clean" option will be added later** (low priority, after Wave 3): a switch, default Classic,
  that corrects VCO-1 at the modulator's edges and tracks the pitch change inside each sample
  (study: −104 dB or better for VCO-2 up to 1 kHz and up to one octave; no cost when off).
- **Two defects found by the study are fixed now, independent of that choice:** hard sync with a
  sine VCO-1 aliases at −77 dB without any cross-mod (a missing slope correction; the sync gate only
  tested a saw); and the sync + cross-mod sound differs between real-time and offline rendering by
  up to 29 dB on single partials, so a bounce does not sound like playback.
- Study findings worth keeping: pure FM bandwidth is negligible at musical settings (sine into sine
  at one octave: about −225 dB); the aliasing comes from the modulator's edges passing through the
  exponential pitch mapping and from assuming a constant pitch within a sample; extra oversampling
  is a poor fix (twice the CPU for −53 to −68 dB); low-passing the modulator dulls the sound.

| Unison voices starting from idle share one base phase per note plus a fixed offset of k²/(2n) cycles for voice k. | With independent random phases, unison at detune 0 had a random level per note (−4.8 to −26.7 dB with 16 voices). This pattern sums to exactly √n, so the level equals one voice at any detune. At detune 0, even harmonics get a fixed colouring. |
| Unison retriggers and returns legato exactly like Solo. | The contract only defined it for Solo; one rule for both mono modes. |
| "A key held" means a physically held key for Legato portamento. For the LFO-delay restart, notes held by the sustain pedal also count. | Legato is a playing technique; but delayed vibrato must not drop out on ringing pedal notes when the next note is played. |
| In Poly, every new note glides from the previously played note. | As specified; chords glide internally when portamento is on, as on the classic. |
| A stolen note keeps its gate length: start and release are both delayed by the 3 ms fade. A steal of a voice whose envelope has already ended starts at once. | A stolen note shorter than 3 ms was silently dropped. |
| Stealing: "oldest in release" is the voice that entered release first; pedal-held voices count as held. Re-pressing a key retriggers its releasing or pedal-held voice. | Simple, deterministic rules. |
| A mode or unison-count change releases all voices and forgets held keys (they must be re-pressed). | Avoids stuck or orphaned notes across a mode change. |
| Idle voices are reported in the order they finished (sample-exact). | "Idle longest" must not depend on block splitting; it changed which voice a note landed on. |
| Envelope key follow follows a legato note change at the event. | Matches a keyboard CV; the times used to change one host block late. |
| Mod wheel: wheel × depth is added to the VCO LFO mod slider before the taper, capped at the slider maximum; it is subject to the LFO delay. | Same control path as the slider. |
| Portamento time applies per block; unison detune, velocity amounts, wheel depth and the wheel are smoothed. Retriggering a sounding voice smooths velocity and unison level over 5 ms. | No zipper noise or gain steps. |
| Pitch glide and drift are computed from absolute time in double precision, rounded once. | Per-sub-block float rounding integrates into phase (−74 dB between block sizes for glide). Now −118 dB or better with 16 voices at full Vintage. |
| Bend range labels are "0 st" … "12 st"; the velocity parameter names contain an arrow (UTF-8, guarded by a test). | Readability; "Velocity to VCA" is the fallback if a host shows it badly. |
| **CPU at high session rates (open):** cost scales with the sample rate. 32 voices at 2× cost 5.0 % of a core at 48 kHz but 18.2 % at 192 kHz, above the 15 % budget, which is defined at 48 kHz. Planned for M3: 1× oversampling when the host rate is 88.2 kHz or higher. | The internal rate is then at least 88.2 kHz at every host rate, which is what the alias gate needs. |

### Effect blocks (review unless marked)
| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Chorus mix curve: wet gain = mix^1.152 (0.45 at the default mix 0.5, 1.0 at full mix). The part of the wet signal that differs between left and right is capped at 0.7, and above the default the output is level-compensated. (strength confirmed by owner) | Full mix would otherwise cancel completely in mono; the worst mono dip at full mix is −12.5 dB, and −5.1 dB at the default. |
| A settled delay time is rounded to a whole sample (echo spacing within ±0.5 sample). | At fractional delays the interpolator darkened every repeat (0.5 dB per repeat at 10 kHz), depending on time × sample rate. |

## Wave 1 — M2a, test infrastructure, CI cost (2026-11-12)

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Fast `exp2` / `tan` / `tanh` approximations in the voice path**, with tested bounds (relative error ≤ 2e-7 / 5e-7, absolute ≤ 2e-7; static pitch error ≤ 0.009 cent). They return NaN for NaN. | Profiling showed the voice is limited by its serial dependency chain per sample, where libm calls cost far more than in isolation. NaN must still reach the output guard. |
| **Voices render sample by sample across all voices** (interleaved, groups of 32) after a vectorised per-voice block-prepare phase; shared modulation is computed once per part in sub-blocks of up to 32 oversampled samples. | Interleaving lets the CPU overlap the voices' serial chains: 32 voices went from 11.3 % to 4.7 % of one core. Fully voice-by-voice rendering measured several times slower. |
| **Parameter smoothing stays per sample (closed form), not control rate.** | Linear control-rate ramps measured −55 dB of error on fast cutoff moves. |
| **Smoothers and the drift walk are kept as target + deviation.** | A plain one-pole stalls short of its target by an amount that depends on the step size, which made renders depend on block splitting and left tiny permanent pitch/cutoff offsets. |
| **Renders are independent of host block size and event positions at any Vintage amount** (tested: −110 dB between block sizes 512 and 500 at Vintage 1). | Offline bounces and golden renders must be reproducible. |
| **All Notes Off (CC 123 and the overflow marker) also clears the sustain pedal;** Reset Controllers clears bend, mod wheel and pedal. | A held pedal must never be able to keep notes hanging after a panic or an event overflow. |
| **Every MIDI event is applied exactly once:** before the block → offset 0, at or after its end → after the last sample, zero-length blocks still apply events. Malformed channel messages are dropped. | A note-off at the block boundary was being lost (possible hung note); masking bad data bytes could turn garbage into All Sound Off. |
| **Golden renders compare third-octave band energy per 25 ms frame, level envelope and tail** (3 dB per cell with ≤ 1 % outliers, 0.4 dB mean, 2 dB level, −80 dB gate). References store features, not audio. | Verified in CI: a reference generated on arm64 matches on x64 and with MSVC. Detects a 3.5 % cutoff change, a missing note or a 25 ms timing change. |
| **VST3 validator: built in CI only** from Steinberg's SDK at `v3.8.1_build_84` (pinned by commit SHA, MIT), cached, run as ctest `vst3_validator` on macOS and Windows x64. The test requires the validator's own "N tests passed, 0 tests failed" line. (owner decision) | JUCE does not bundle the validator; nothing is added to the repo or linked into the plugin. Exit code alone passed in 0.2 s and proved nothing. |
| **CI cost control while the repo is private:** docs-only changes don't build; draft PRs build Windows x64 only, the full matrix runs when a PR is marked ready, on `main` and on manual dispatch; 30-minute job timeout; Dependabot monthly. (review) | macOS minutes are billed at a multiple; MSVC is the one thing that cannot be checked locally. To be relaxed when the repo goes public (free standard runners). |
| **Push discipline:** one push per branch, after local tests, a local AddressSanitizer/UBSan run for engine changes, and the review fixes. Docs are batched with code. | 13 CI runs on the first orchestrated day, several of them avoidable. |
| **Effects (owner feedback and review):** chorus full mix reaches wet ≈ dry, with the default at the earlier maximum; no bulk clearing on the audio thread; reported tail capped at 30 s; level 0 fades out in 20 ms and drops the tail (review). | See `docs/architecture.md` section 5. |

## Wave 0 — contracts for M2–M5 (2026-11-12)

The interfaces are in `docs/architecture.md`. These are the judgement calls behind them.
All are marked **(review)**: the owner has not confirmed them yet.

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Stolen voices fade out over 3 ms, then start the new note** (the note starts 3 ms late). No spare voices for the fade. (review) | Click-free with a fixed 16 voices and a predictable CPU cost. 3 ms is inside the 2–5 ms spec and below audible timing error. |
| **Steal order:** oldest voice in release, else oldest held voice, never the newest. (review) | Releasing voices are the least audible to lose; protecting the newest note keeps the melody intact. |
| **Unison:** 2/4/8/16 voices (default 4), total detune spread 0–50 ct, output scaled by 1/√n. (review) | A selectable count keeps CPU in the user's hands; 1/√n keeps loudness steady when the count changes. |
| **Portamento is an RC glide in pitch**, time = 99 % of the interval, modes Always/Legato. (review) | Matches the analog behaviour and the envelope time definition already used. |
| **Velocity:** two per-part amounts, to VCA gain (default 0.5) and to VCF envelope depth (default 0). (review) | The classic had no velocity; a modest VCA default suits modern keyboards, and amount 0 restores the original behaviour. |
| **Sustain pedal (CC 64) and mod wheel vibrato (CC 1) are part of M2.** (review) | A polysynth without a sustain pedal is not playable; the wheel maps to the classic bender-section LFO vibrato. |
| **Bend range per part, 0–12 semitones, default 2.** | Replaces the fixed ±2 from M1, as planned. |
| **Parts are mono up to the mixer; stereo comes from pan and effects.** No per-voice unison stereo spread. (review) | One decimator per part instead of two: the decimator is a fixed per-part cost in a tight CPU budget. |
| **Effects are global, in series on the stereo bus:** chorus → delay → reverb. Per-part sends are deferred. (review) | Simplest structure that gives the classic sound; per-part sends can be appended later without breaking IDs. |
| **Whole mode plays the Lower part only** (16 voices). (review) | Simple and predictable; a 32-voice single patch is not needed for the CPU target. |
| **MIDI is parsed once into plain `NoteEvent`s**; parts and the arpeggiator work on those. Fixed buffer; overflow drops the rest of the block and sends All Notes Off. | Lets the arp/sequencer be a pure, testable event transformer, and keeps `juce::MidiMessage` off the audio thread. Overflow can never hang a note. |
| **CPU optimisation is part of M2**, target ≤ 0.30 % of a core per voice. | The extended benchmark measures 0.35 % per voice, so 32 voices ≈ 11.5 % of the 15 % budget. M2 adds per-voice work and the effects need about 1.5 %, so some margin has to be won back. (The earlier 0.47 % figure was a single unrepeated run and was not reproduced.) |
| **Golden renders compare perceptual features, not samples.** | arm64/x64 and clang/MSVC math libraries differ in the last bits; a self-oscillating filter amplifies that. |

## M1 — single-part voice engine (2026-11-12)

### Panel interpretation
| Panel element | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sync switch | **VCO-1 is the slave and is hard-synced to VCO-2.** | Matches the classic architecture, where VCO-2 is the modulator for both Cross Mod and Sync, and VCO-2's Lo Freq mode makes it a usable sync/FM master. The parameter ID stays `vco2.sync` because the switch sits in the VCO-2 section. |
| Cross Mod | VCO-2 → VCO-1 **exponential** audio-rate FM, max ±4 oct (squared taper). | Classic analog cross-mod behaviour; the squared taper gives fine control at low settings. |
| VCO-2 Lo Freq | VCO-2 stops tracking the keyboard and pitch bend and runs at 1–8 Hz (16'..2'), adjusted by Fine Tune. VCO pitch mods still apply. | Turns VCO-2 into a second, sync-capable LFO. |
| Waveforms | Both VCOs offer Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, Pulse, Noise. | The reference layout is ambiguous per VCO, so a superset costs nothing. (confirmed by owner 2026-11-12) |
| VCO ranges | VCO-1: 32'..1' (6 steps). VCO-2: 16'..2' (4 steps). 8' = keyboard pitch. | As printed on the panel. |

### Ranges and tapers (`source/common/params/Mappings.h`)
These are the single source of truth: the parameter display text and the DSP both call these functions.

- VCF cutoff 20 Hz – 20.48 kHz, exponential (10 octaves). Env mod up to 7 oct. LFO mod up to ±4 oct (squared taper). Key follow 0–100 %.
- Resonance k = 4.15 × slider. It self-oscillates from about 96 %.
- Envelope times 1 ms – 10 s, exponential. Attack time = time to reach the peak (RC charging toward 1.3).
  Decay and release time = time to cover 99 % of the way to the target.
- LFO rate 0.05 – 40 Hz. LFO delay 0 – 5 s (squared taper).
- VCO pitch mod: LFO up to ±2 oct and ENV up to +4 oct, both squared taper. Fine tune ±1 semitone.
- VCA level is squared (perceptually even).
- Pitch bend is fixed at ±2 semitones for now. It becomes a setting in M2/M3.

### DSP architecture
- **Oscillator anti-aliasing: windowed-sinc BLEP/BLAMP table** (16 taps, Kaiser β = 8, cutoff 0.45 × oversampled rate)
  instead of 2-point PolyBLEP. PolyBLEP measured only −58 to −65 dB alias at 1–2 kHz fundamentals at 2×.
  The table version measures **≤ −113 dB for every wave up to 5 kHz**, and the test gate is set at −100 dB.
  The cost is 16 multiply-adds per waveform edge plus 8 oversampled samples of delay (4 base samples).
- **Oversampling: 2× real-time, 4× offline** (`isNonRealtime()` at prepare time). It uses JUCE's polyphase IIR
  decimator with a custom stage (transition 0.05, −100 dB). Voices render directly at the oversampled
  rate; the "upsampling" pass is fed silence, because JUCE's API needs it before decimating.
- **Latency is reported** to the host (decimator + BLEP delay, about 7 samples at 2×).
- **VCF: ZDF 4-pole OTA cascade.** The feedback is solved linearly, then passed through tanh at the cascade input.
  There's no iterative solve. Tests confirm exact cutoff (−12.04 dB / −6.02 dB at fc in the linear region),
  correct slopes, and self-oscillation at fc ±3 %.
- **Parameter handling: a single X-macro list** (`PartParamList.h`) generates the snapshot struct, the
  APVTS layout for both parts, the atomic binding and the per-sample smoothing (5 ms one-pole).
  Switches are applied per block without smoothing.
- **Both parts' parameters exist now** (`lower.*` and `upper.*`); only Lower is played until M3.
  Parameters can't be added later without appending, so defining the full set now keeps the order logical.
- **Vintage** (global, default 0 = clean). Per voice it controls static VCO offsets (±6 ct), slow pitch drift (±3 ct),
  cutoff offset (±0.12 oct) and envelope time spread (±8 %). It's seeded per voice, so renders are deterministic.
- **Mono note handling in M1**: last-note priority. Envelopes retrigger on each new note; returning to a held
  note changes pitch without a retrigger. M2 replaces this with the polyphonic allocator.

- Test-only renders go to `$VS_RENDER_DIR`, because `build/` is ignored by git.
